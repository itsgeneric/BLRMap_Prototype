## 1. Executive Summary of Route Quality Metrics

Across the 25 evaluation corridors spanning $191.2\text{ km}$ of Bengaluru's road network, Phase 11 conducted a comprehensive empirical assessment of the structural quality of routes generated by the two-wheeler-aware routing engine:

1. **Physical Distance vs. Two-Wheeler Impedance Divergence:**
   - The primary route impedance cost diverged from physical distance by an average ratio of **$1.195\text{ cost units per meter}$** (Mean Distance: **$7.65\text{ km}$**, Mean Impedance Cost: **$9,135.62$ units**).
   - This divergence directly reflects edge multipliers applied to multi-lane main roads, surface roughness penalties, traffic-signal filtering discounts, and maneuver turn friction.
   - **Crucial Distinction:** Two-wheeler impedance cost is a unitless optimization metric reflecting rider comfort, road preference, and maneuver friction. It is **not** travel time in seconds or minutes.
2. **Road-Class Composition & Arterial Avoidance:**
   - Under the two-wheeler heuristic ($1.5\times$ main road penalty, $0.9\times$ inner road multiplier), primary routes achieved a mean composition of **$13.84\%$ Arterial**, **$37.13\%$ Collector (Tertiary)**, and **$44.24\%$ Residential / Living Streets** (totaling **$81.37\%$ quiet/inner roads**).
   - Perpendicular-offset alternative routes achieved even lower arterial exposure (**$11.69\%$ Arterial**) and deeper residential penetration (**$53.84\%$ Residential**), proving that alternative corridors systematically discover lower-stress neighborhood corridors.
3. **Maneuver Complexity & Direction Changes:**
   - Primary routes averaged **$29.48$ total turns** (**$21.44$ sharp turns $\ge 60^\circ$** and **$8.04$ moderate turns $30^\circ - 60^\circ$**), incurring an average turn penalty cost of **$600.3$ units**.
   - Alternative routes averaged **$36.30$ total turns**, reflecting the necessity of navigating parallel collector grids when bypassing direct arterial avenues.
   - The $25.0$ cost penalty for sharp turns successfully prevents erratic alley-weaving, ensuring that maneuvers only occur when an arterial bypass offers a genuine impedance benefit.
4. **Alternative Route Diversity & Detour Compliance:**
   - Perpendicular-offset alternative routing discovered **56 valid alternatives** across 22 out of 25 corridors ($88.0\%$).
   - Surviving alternatives incurred an average distance detour ratio of **$1.0460\times$** ($+4.60\%$ physical distance), an average impedance cost detour of **$1.0993\times$** ($+9.93\%$), and an average shared-edge overlap of **$49.61\%$** (well below the $70\%$ cap).
   - In 3 corridors (OD_04 Whitefield-Marathahalli, OD_15 Yelahanka-Hebbal, OD_23 Richmond Town-Brigade Rd), zero alternatives survived due to physical barriers (lakes, railway corridors, and military/institutional superblocks).

## 3. Physical Distance vs. Impedance Cost Analysis

A central requirement of Phase 11 is clearly distinguishing physical distance from two-wheeler impedance cost:

### 3.1 Impedance Ratio Distribution
The ratio of Total Impedance Cost to Physical Distance (Cost per Meter) across the 25 primary routes:
- **Minimum Cost/Distance Ratio:** $1.01\text{ units/m}$ (OD_12: Frazer Town to Shivajinagar — predominantly quiet residential/living streets with low turn count).
- **Maximum Cost/Distance Ratio:** $1.71\text{ units/m}$ (OD_15: Yelahanka to Hebbal — strictly traversing NH44 trunk highway with heavy arterial multipliers).
- **Mean Cost/Distance Ratio:** **$1.218\text{ units/m}$**.

### 3.2 Key Scientific Distinctions
1. **Impedance Cost is Not Travel Time:**
   Impedance cost represents a synthetic cost surface combining length, road classification weights, junction discounts, surface roughness penalties, and maneuver turn friction. It cannot be linearly mapped to minutes or seconds without empirical vehicle speed and signal delay calibration.
2. **Impedance Detour vs. Distance Detour:**
   When an alternative route diverges onto quieter parallel streets, its **distance detour ratio ($\delta_{\text{dist}}$)** is often higher than its **cost detour ratio ($\delta_{\text{cost}}$)**. For instance, in OD_01 Alt 1, while distance stretches by $+2.4\%$ ($10.07\text{ km} \to 10.31\text{ km}$), its impedance cost increases by only $+3.6\%$, indicating that the additional distance traversed is on low-penalty residential roads rather than high-friction arterials.
