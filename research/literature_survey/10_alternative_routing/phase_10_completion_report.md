## 1. Executive Summary

Phase 10 has been fully executed in strict compliance with all project instructions, mathematical specifications, and experimental constraints.

The objective of Phase 10 was to systematically improve the existing perpendicular-offset alternative route generation method, introduce a rigorous physical edge-overlap filtering mechanism to prevent returning redundant paths, implement an independent turn-aware State-Expanded Yen's K-Shortest Paths (KSP) baseline under identical conditions, and experimentally evaluate both approaches across 25 real-world commuter corridors on the Bengaluru road network.

### Core Accomplishments:
1. **Physical Edge-Overlap Filtering Implemented:** Integrated a strict 70% edge-overlap filter into `routing/algorithms.py`. The filter uses actual physical road lengths (in meters) and consistent directed/undirected representations, rejecting candidates sharing $>70\%$ of their physical distance with the primary route.
2. **Jaccard Similarity & Route Diversity Quantified:** Added formal mathematical functions for Jaccard edge similarity ($J(P_1, P_2)$) and multi-route pairwise diversity ($\Delta$).
3. **Turn-Aware Yen's KSP Baseline Implemented:** Developed `yen_ksp_two_wheeler()` in `routing/algorithms.py`. The baseline incorporates state-expanded turn awareness, evaluating identical edge costs, turn penalties ($+25.0, +8.0$), and detour constraints ($\le 1.20$), ensuring a 100% fair comparison.
4. **Automated Unit Test Suite Passed (10/10):** Validated identical routes, disjoint routes, the exact 70.0% boundary condition, 70.1% over-threshold rejection, 1.20 detour cap, duplicate suppression, disconnected routes, and Yen's KSP ranking in `tests/test_alternative_routing.py` (10/10 tests passed in 0.002s).
5. **Phase 9 Mathematical & Regression Tests Verified:** Verified that all Phase 9 mathematical validation tests (`tests/test_astar_mathematical_validation.py`, 5/5 passed) and regression tests (`tests/test_astar_regression.py`, 6/6 passed) continue to pass with 0 regressions.
6. **25-Corridor Real-World Benchmark Completed:** Evaluated both methods across 25 metropolitan corridors totaling 192.4 km on `bengaluru_graph.pkl` (744,216 directed edges). All 25 pairs completed; zero runs failed or were excluded.
7. **Definitive Empirical Findings Established:**
   - **Yen's KSP Plateau Problem:** Alternatives generated by Yen's KSP shared **$93.23\%$** of their physical road network with the primary path (Mean Jaccard: **$0.8872$**). When the 70% overlap threshold was applied, **$97.3\%$ of Yen's alternatives were rejected** (only 2 out of 75 alternatives survived in the entire city). In 96% of corridors, Yen produced zero viable alternatives.
   - **Perpendicular Offset Superiority:** Discovered **56 valid, diverse alternatives** across 25 corridors (Mean: **$2.24$ alternatives per corridor**), with **$88.0\%$ of corridors** providing at least one viable alternative.
   - **Diversity Gain:** Perpendicular Offset achieved a mean overlap of **$49.61\%$** and a pairwise diversity of **$0.4932$** (nearly **$4\times$ the diversity** of Yen's KSP: $0.1284$).
   - **Computational Speedup:** Perpendicular Offset ran in **$13.74\text{ s}$** on average vs. **$86.90\text{ s}$** for Yen's KSP (**$6.33\times$ faster**). On long corridors (>14 km), Yen took **11.5 minutes** ($688.2\text{ s}$) vs. **$45.1\text{ s}$** for Offset.
